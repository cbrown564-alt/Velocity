## Separate failure and burden measures

- **Missed mandatory findings:** M3 in every SBT-004 arm; none in SBT-005/006 under provisional adjudication.
- **Unsupported selected claims:** none identified in these twelve semantic reviews; this is not a calibrated error-rate estimate.
- **Wrong bases or estimands:** none identified in selected claims checked; routed and all-assigned sensitivities remain qualified. Numeric audits cover explicit mapped estimates and selected prose, not every number or possible interpretation.
- **Inappropriate promotion of weak results:** no certain optimum, tiny-cell targeting, receipt causality or actual-economic claim identified. The frozen restraints and selected claim strength remain inspectable separately.
- **Technical failures:** one retained model timeout, followed by one same-configuration replacement. Trace warnings and denied runtime/cache writes are retained. Recorded actions show no hidden-reference or external data reads; this is not a claim of complete operating-system isolation.
- **Correction burden and preference:** human active minutes, human correction counts and calibrated dimension scores remain null. Proposed agent corrections are separate fields, not measured reviewer effort.

A single replicate per arm is not E5 reliability. Longer staged runs cannot establish a quality advantage from these pairs; mandatory recall is unchanged within every study. This pilot compares workflows under one model/configuration and does not rank models. Reported token totals count the completed CLI turns, including cached inputs; failed-attempt tokens were not reported. Monetary cost is unavailable and remains null, not zero. See the comparison for exact timings and usage; summed run time is not elapsed wall time because some runs overlapped.

## Limits and next decisions

SBT-004 v1 prefilters the current-tool-use table before building eligible-base metadata, leaving its DK/refusal breakdown unavailable in that table. The analysis model noticed this; v1 is preserved and any repair must create a new version. The one-reference-per-finding score also undercredits some bundled secondary content. Researchers should assess whether composite secondary requirements are proportionate to the management decision before a new benchmark version changes them.

All three projects are exposed development/evaluation cases with training prohibited. No untouched holdout, independent domain validation, actual-market conclusion or transfer beyond the earlier published FSA tables is established. The next evidence is the reviewers' findings, preferences and measured correction burden; then correct identified study issues in new versions, test a study-neutral completeness check, and repeat promising configurations. Fine-tuning and durable product promotion remain deferred until those comparisons show persistent weaknesses and a useful researcher experience.
