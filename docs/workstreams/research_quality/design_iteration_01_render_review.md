# SBT-001 Design Iteration 01 — Render Review

## Scope

Slides 1–3 were implemented as an editable PowerPoint prototype and rendered before extending the visual system.

## Iteration history

### Render 01

- Slide 1 passed structural review.
- Slide 2 failed: the long executive headline wrapped into the deck/subhead region, weakening hierarchy and creating a visible collision.
- Slide 3 passed geometry review: the position × momentum plot rendered as continuous axes/grid lines with direct labels and no broken series/connector problem.

### Render 02

Slide 2 was rewritten rather than squeezed typographically:

- headline changed to **“Three pressures are reshaping the category.”**
- the three-brand synthesis moved into the deck line;
- evidence rows remain the body of the executive argument.

This restored a clean headline → deck → evidence hierarchy.

## What now passes

### Slide 1 — category thesis

- headline dominates at thumbnail scale;
- three narrative signposts read as subordinate editorial cues rather than dashboard cards;
- whitespace feels intentional;
- the warm paper field + thin Velocity accent provides identity without overwhelming the research.

### Slide 2 — executive synthesis

- headline no longer competes with the evidence rows;
- one integrated argument reads vertically;
- quantitative anchors and interpretation are visibly different layers;
- Pulse is slightly more emphatic without making Northstar/Harbour decorative;
- no questionnaire-order or KPI-card treatment.

### Slide 3 — competitive landscape

- position and momentum are encoded on explicit quantitative axes;
- zero-change reference is subordinate but visible;
- Pulse is the dominant mark; Northstar is the positional anchor;
- all five brands are directly labelled;
- commentary rail adds the distinction between position and momentum rather than repeating coordinates;
- no broken chart-line geometry.

## Remaining refinements before propagating

The opening system is good enough to continue, but the following should remain active design checks:

1. Avoid turning the right-side commentary rail into a repeated template on later slides.
2. Use semantic colour sparingly; Northstar red should mean customer deterioration, not simply “Northstar brand colour.”
3. Do not force every slide into the same headline/deck/footer proportions if the visual argument benefits from a different rhythm.
4. For trend slides, prefer native charts or a single continuous path per series and render-test every annotation.
5. Slide 4 should establish the deck's hero-chart treatment; slide 5 should deliberately use a different visual grammar (segment comparison) to test consistency without sameness.

## Decision

**Opening visual system accepted for continuation.**

Proceed to slides 4–6 as the Pulse chapter: breakout → younger audience → conversion challenge. Do not yet propagate to Northstar/Harbour until the Pulse chapter has also passed rendered QA.
