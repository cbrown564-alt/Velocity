# Publication-quality visual standard

Turn 6 v1 was intentionally rejected as below the desired bar. It was cleaner than mechanical research automation, but still behaved too much like a presentation template: oversized cards, excessive whitespace without information hierarchy, generic callouts, and charts that looked like slide widgets rather than editorial graphics.

## Reference direction

The revised standard takes its strongest cues from Pew Research Center's editorial graphics and the Financial Times visual vocabulary.

Pew's own 2025 visualization review says its graphics are designed to communicate findings clearly and concisely, have clear takeaways, engage readers, work on small screens, and ultimately tell a story about the research. It highlights adding context directly to familiar charts with dots, lines and annotations, and using compact forms such as bullet charts when they improve comparison rather than for novelty.

The FT visual vocabulary reinforces choosing chart form from the analytical relationship — change over time, comparison, distribution, relationship, part-to-whole — rather than from a fixed dashboard component library.

## Velocity v2 rules

1. **Editorial graphic, not dashboard tile.** Charts should occupy the page naturally and be composed with commentary, not placed inside generic rounded cards.
2. **Finding headline + explanatory dek.** The title states the insight; the dek explains the analytical frame or qualification.
3. **Direct labeling by default.** Put brand/series names and terminal values next to the evidence. Avoid legends when direct labels work.
4. **Annotations live beside evidence.** A caveat about weighting, universe or interpretation sits next to the chart it changes.
5. **Comparison context is visual.** Use reference dots, paired values, slopes, direct contrasts and small multiples rather than paragraphs explaining what the reader could have seen.
6. **Shared scales for comparable small multiples.** Avoid visually exaggerating small movements by autoscaling every series independently.
7. **Colour carries meaning.** Most marks are neutral. Accent colour identifies the story; red is reserved for deterioration/risk, not decoration.
8. **No ornamental containers.** Boxes, pills and cards require a semantic purpose. Whitespace and rules should create structure first.
9. **Footnotes are part of the graphic.** Universe, weighting and source notes are small but readable and consistent.
10. **Narrative flow alternates context and focus.** Establish category shape, zoom into the important dynamics, demonstrate restraint, then close on implications.
11. **Restraint is visible.** Publication quality includes what is deliberately omitted; low-base or weak evidence should not be rescued by attractive design.
12. **Native editability remains required.** Publication quality cannot come at the cost of turning evidence into screenshots.

## V2 reference-deck changes

- replaced three-card executive summary with an editorial three-row readout;
- replaced generic chart cards with direct-labelled lines and shared visual scales;
- moved qualifications into deks/adjacent commentary;
- separated total-market and subgroup Pulse trends while keeping a common scale;
- framed Northstar as a divergence between customer NPS and market awareness;
- made Harbour's long-run slope the visual argument rather than a KPI card;
- simplified the restraint slide into an editorial contrast;
- made the final slide a short decision hierarchy rather than a generic recommendations page.

## Future benchmark implication

E6 should not reward surface polish alone. Automated checks should score layout integrity and evidence fidelity, while expert pairwise review should ask whether the page behaves like a finished research publication: clear takeaway, appropriate chart grammar, useful annotation, disciplined density, and a coherent place in the report's argument.
