# Quantitative researchers at work: implications for Velocity

**Research note, 26 September 2026.** Desk research and product inference, not user research. Companion to [the nine interaction sketches](plan_07_researcher_journey_and_output_quality.md#researcher-workflow-exploration--26-september-2026) in draft PR #80. This note records where the sketches match observed practice, where competitors already serve the need, and what to test with researchers. Sources and limitations follow.

## The short answer

The quantitative/qualitative distinction still describes methods, skills and often job titles, but it is not a clean division of people or agencies. In Greenbook's 2026 GRIT sample of **brand-side researchers**, 45% said all their projects were quantitative, 24% all qualitative, and 28% involved both (n=170). The question is about the allocation of their projects, not the composition of the entire industry or a census of agency job titles. Among brand-side analytics professionals the mixed share was 39% (n=148). A quant specialist may work with qualitative colleagues, open ends, behavioural or operational data, and client strategists without personally moderating interviews. [GRIT data](https://www.greenbook.org/grit/insights-practice-edition/ai-readable).

Agencies remain significant. In the same GRIT sample, 84% of brand-side researchers reported working at least occasionally with full-service research suppliers and 83% with qualitative suppliers; these are **incidence of relationships**, not shares of projects or revenue. ESOMAR's 2025 buyers research places in-house execution around half for several team structures (52% centralized, 49% hybrid, 50% decentralized), with substantial variation by industry: 30% of FMCG and 23% of pharmaceutical projects in house in its breakdown. Brand tracking, international reach, hard-to-reach audiences, advanced methods, scale and independence still pull work toward external partners. Treat the in-house/agency boundary as a handoff and collaboration pattern, not a binary market choice. [GRIT data](https://www.greenbook.org/grit/insights-practice-edition/ai-readable); [ESOMAR make-or-buy analysis](https://researchworld.com/articles/in-house-or-agency-how-insights-leaders-are-redrawing-the-line-in-2025); [ESOMAR internalisation analysis](https://researchworld.com/our-members-stories/how-is-research-internalisation-defined-in-2025).

**Proposed positioning:** a *research decision workspace* for survey teams who receive messy study files, need to establish what a number means, investigate a business question, defend a finding, and keep the answer current. This is a hypothesis about value, not a claim of unique functionality. The product should prove that it reduces consequential review and rework while retaining researcher control. “AI that automatically cleans, analyzes and writes a traceable editable deck” is already a competitor claim.

## Who does what

| Role in a project | Actual responsibility | What Velocity can help with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Client or internal stakeholder | Frames a decision, asks for explanations and action, may change the question after seeing findings | Preserve original brief, operational research questions, scope and a portable answer with caveats |
| Research lead / client service quant researcher | Proposal, questionnaire and analysis plan, suppliers, timing and budget, interpretation, client presentation | Reviewable study definition, judgment and narrative; direct response to follow-up questions |
| Survey programmer and field / sample operations | Implement routing, test instrument, recruit, monitor quotas and fraud | Import survey and provenance metadata; surface limits that affect interpretation. No near-term claim to run fieldwork |
| Data processing / analytics specialist | Clean and label file, code, weight, recode, tabulate, test, produce tables | Inspect source versus analysis state; explicit universes, transformations, weighted bases and reproducible calculations |
| Senior reviewer / methods lead | Challenge bases, exclusions, comparisons, uncertainty, confounds and wording | Review only decisions affected by an analytical change; record accepted and rejected claims |
| Client-side insights partner | Commission or do research, relate results to prior waves, internal data and organizational decisions | Reuse approved findings and answer what changed; allow agency-to-client handoff without severing evidence |

These roles overlap in a small agency and split into specialist teams in larger ones. Ipsos job descriptions independently show client-service executives designing instruments, managing projects, reporting and presenting, while data-processing executives script questionnaires, use SPSS/Dimensions/Excel, QA data and deliver tables. A senior research executive's remit includes table specifications, analysis plan, report writing and client presentation. These are examples of work allocation, not representative prevalence estimates. [Client service](https://www.ipsos.com/ro-ro/client-service-executive-quantitative-research); [processing](https://www.ipsos.com/ro-ro/data-processing-scripting-exec); [senior researcher](https://www.ipsos.com/en-za/senior-research-executive).

## The loop as experienced by a quant researcher

1. **Win and scope.** Turn a client's business problem into a measurable study, budget, timeline and deliverable. Decide whether existing evidence suffices. Trade off sample, speed, precision and depth. Much of this work is conversation, Word, slides and spreadsheets.
2. **Design and program.** Questionnaire, routing, response options, quotas, table specification and analysis plan are passed among client, researcher, programmer and field teams. Wording and routing changes can break comparability with prior waves.
3. **Field and check.** Monitor sample composition, quotas, speeders, duplicates, straightlining, suspicious respondents, missingness and open ends. Quality decisions involve evidence and judgment; a tidy dataset is not necessarily valid. GRIT says fraud detection is routinely used across segments; that is a signal of the problem's salience, not proof that a particular tool solves it. [GRIT](https://www.greenbook.org/grit/insights-practice-edition/ai-readable).
4. **Receive and reconcile.** A researcher may get SAV/CSV/Excel, a questionnaire, codebook, DP notes, weighting specification, prior-wave data, a table book and a changed client brief. The same variable can have a terse name, a long label, and a subtly different universe. This is a strong proposed Velocity entry point; frequency of file mismatch still needs observation.
5. **Prepare and analyse.** Check routing and valid codes; set populations, nets, derived measures, weights and comparison bases. Run toplines and banner tables, inspect small bases and uncertainty, investigate subgroup differences, look for alternative explanations, and compare waves. The result is often a collection of tables and candidate claims rather than a linear “analysis complete” state.
6. **Build and review a story.** Select findings that answer the decision; distinguish observation, interpretation and recommendation. Recheck every figure, base and source question in a deck or workbook, then revise with colleagues. The reviewer may reject a technically correct but misleading claim.
7. **Present and respond.** A client asks about a different segment, the unweighted result, changed data, a previous wave or a caveat. The team must re-run and sometimes retract a line of prose. The answer should survive this loop without losing what was decided and why.

This sequence is synthesized from observed job responsibilities, vendors' documented capabilities and professional disclosure requirements. It is a **workflow model to validate**, not a measured time-use study. AAPOR's June 2026 code requires enough detail for independent review, including sampling, mode, field dates, weighting, processing, quality checks, exclusions and AI use. That makes source-to-claim traceability a professional obligation in many survey contexts, though specific client research may follow different standards. [AAPOR code](https://aapor.org/standards-and-ethics/).

| AI assistance | Cleaning, analysis, coding and reporting increasingly supported by agents | This category is moving quickly. Vendor documentation shows supported features, not typical use, reliability or researcher satisfaction. Displayr already says its agent handles complex survey structures, computes statistics, traces results to raw data, accepts briefs, writes editable reports, exports PowerPoint and supports rerunning waves. Its FAQ says automatic report updates can overwrite edits, hence explicit reruns. Velocity cannot differentiate simply by adding these feature labels. Test the actual interaction and exception paths against current product, then compare task outcomes rather than marketing screenshots. [Displayr Research Agent](https://www.displayr.com/research-agent/); [Forsta](https://www.forsta.com/platform/market-research/data-visualization/).

## How the work feels, and what the evidence really says

The defensible synthesis is **pressure for speed paired with anxiety about whether the evidence and story can be trusted**. IntelliSurvey's practitioner report landing page describes declining data quality, AI uncertainty and being asked to do more faster; the full report is gated, so it supplies neither a usable estimate nor an independently checked ranking. Qualtrics' 2025 survey of 3,000+ researchers in 14 countries reports 89% using AI regularly or experimentally and 45% citing bots/fraud in quant as a reason digital qual is more appealing. That is a vendor survey and blends experimental with regular use; its prediction that synthetic responses would dominate within three years is **respondent expectation**, not an observed market share. [IntelliSurvey](https://www.intellisurvey.com/2025-market-research-industry-pulse-report); [Qualtrics](https://www.qualtrics.com/news/ai-to-drive-massive-changes-to-market-research-in-2025-qualtrics-report-says/).

GRIT captures ambivalence: among the smaller brand-researcher agentic-AI subsample (n=88), 57% mostly/completely agree non-users risk being left behind, while 44% mostly/completely agree the discussion dramatically overstates adoption and impact. In its broader brand-researcher sample, 42% report formal AI rules, 39% informal ones and 16% none (n=176). These are attitudes and self-reports, not a causal finding about job security or tool performance. [GRIT](https://www.greenbook.org/grit/insights-practice-edition/ai-readable).

Likely moments of frustration to investigate: repeatedly rediscovering a base; reconciling late files; explaining a percentage whose source is hard to locate; checking hundreds of tables for a few consequential changes; defending an interpretation to a skeptical client; and rebuilding a deck after a new wave. The sources establish these as plausible tasks and vendor priorities; **their incidence, time cost and emotional intensity in Velocity's target segment are unmeasured**. Ask researchers about a specific recent project and inspect their artifacts rather than asking which AI features sound useful.

## Implications for the nine prototypes and product direction

| Sketch / capability | Researcher job and differentiation test | Risk to test |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Study map + preparation microscope | Resolve questionnaire, variable, routing, missing-code and DP-note disagreements at import; preview how a proposed correction changes live results | Inferred semantics could create false confidence; require source pointers and reversible changes |
| Population lens + measure studio | State the target population, analysis base, exclusions, measure and weight as reusable objects attached to a result | Added modeling effort may slow common tasks; compare with experienced Q/Displayr users |
| Assumption lab | Put defensible alternative cuts next to the chosen claim; show whether interpretation survives | Automatic challenges may be noisy or invite inappropriate multiple testing |
| Question desk **or** reactive notebook | Keep a brief, hypothesis, analyses, candidate claims, disconfirming evidence and researcher judgment together | Do not force a new workflow vocabulary before the researcher can make a useful table; test both as alternatives |
| Signal inbox | Direct attention to material exceptions and changing findings after a revised file/wave | “Interesting” rankings may miss client context; expose selection criteria and false negatives |
| Living answer + wave rehearsal | Preserve edited prose and approval; mark affected claims stale when evidence changes; replay analysis recipes with explicit mappings | Competitive overlap is substantial; measure rework and safe review, not the presence of update buttons |

The promising **wedge** is a supplied data file plus questionnaire and a real client question. Within one session, a researcher should identify the valid universe, inspect an apparent subgroup difference, test an alternative explanation, approve a defensible statement and export a checkable artifact. Then change the weight or import the corrected file and watch only the affected decisions re-enter review. This exercises Velocity's existing statistical/export foundation and PR #79's offline evidence-bound handoff without pretending those are production-integrated today.

Lead with the researcher's competence: *“Get from a messy survey file to an answer you can explain and revise.”* Avoid “replace the researcher,” “instant insights,” or “first auditable AI agent.” The product promise should include visible unanswered questions and explicit human judgment. Position agencies as the first likely design partner: they repeat the file-to-client loop, work across clients and must defend findings. This is an inference to validate with willingness to adopt, procurement and confidentiality interviews; brand teams are a second segment, especially where they mix in-house work with agencies.

## Next validation, before promotion to production

1. **Observe 8–12 recent-project walkthroughs**, recruiting small/mid agency quant leads, processing specialists, brand-side researchers and one mixed-methods researcher. Ask for redacted questionnaire, raw/clean files, tabs, deck, revision notes and the latest client follow-up. Seek counterexamples where Velocity's proposed layer adds overhead. This sample is for discovery, not prevalence estimation.
2. **Map actual handoffs and time.** For each project, record tools/files, role ownership, changes after initial data, manual checks and the three most consequential errors or near misses. Distinguish task duration from elapsed waiting and from avoidable rework.
3. **Run task comparisons.** Give practitioners the same realistic dataset/question in their current stack and in the prototypes. Measure correct base/weight, valid claim, meaningful caveat, time to defensible answer, count of document switches, reviewer correction burden, and successful response to a changed wave. Log false assurances and unsupported suggestions.
4. **Use two adversarial studies.** One routed brand tracker with overlapping codes and a changed weighting file; one mixed quant/open-end study with ambiguous wording, a small subgroup and a plausible but spurious headline. The frozen SBT studies can supply arithmetic checks, but test usability with researcher-authored or permissioned authentic artifacts.
5. **Competitive reality check.** Let current Displayr/Q and Forsta practitioners attempt the same change-and-review task. Observe their actual workflows and output, including what is already easy. Ask what they would keep using even if Velocity worked well.
6. **Decision gate.** Promote the smallest useful contract only if users can complete a real answer with fewer consequential corrections or materially less rework, without hiding bases, weights, uncertainty or researcher authorship. If not, refine the workflow or narrow to a preparation/review integration.

## Evidence limits

- GRIT 2026 is an industry survey (735 total; 328 brand, 387 supplier, 20 other) with much smaller cells for some comparisons. Its sample is North America heavy; it is not a probability estimate of the global workforce. Percentage denominators vary by table. We have preserved them where used.
- ESOMAR percentages come from its buyers research summaries; the full underlying dashboard/methodology was not inspected here. “Projects internalised” and “supplier relationships” are different measures.
- Job ads and vendor feature pages reveal workflows and commercial positioning, not representative adoption or delivered product quality.
- No first-hand Velocity target-researcher interviews, observed work sessions, independent competitor hands-on tests or measured task-time savings were performed for this note. The prioritization above is a product hypothesis, with a concrete way to falsify it.
