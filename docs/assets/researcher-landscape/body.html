Section 00 · Summary

The hard part is not the first answer. It is keeping the answer true.

Researchers have adopted AI almost universally, but they have not handed it the responsibility for a number. The durable pain is being able to say what a figure means, why it changed, and whether the conclusion still stands — as a study moves between people, tools, and revisions.

The question to test next

Can one researcher carry a defensible answer through a change — a new population, a corrected weight, a suspect batch, a new wave — with less reconstruction and less risk than they can today?

Section 01 · The people around a number

The quant–qual split still exists. It is just not where the work divides.

Specialist roles persist, especially in large agencies. But a finding passes through several hands, and in smaller teams one person holds several of them.

Specialists are real. MRS maintains separate career pathways, and Ipsos advertises roles explicitly for quantitative research, survey scripting, and data processing. A current Ipsos client-service role spans questionnaire checks, sample monitoring, validation, Excel/SPSS preparation, and client deliverables; a separate processing role scripts surveys and produces tables.27 Practitioner voice

But researchers’ projects mix methods. In Greenbook’s 2026 GRIT sample of brand-side researchers, fewer than half said all their projects were quantitative, and more than a quarter said their projects involved both.10 This describes the allocation of their projects, not the make-up of the industry.

Responsibilities blur while organisations don’t converge. GRIT 2026 describes an industry “splitting into two operating models”: analytics professionals are expanding on every tracked dimension, while traditional research functions face budget pressure and a narrowing method portfolio.10 Its commentary reports blurred responsibilities without evidence that insights and analytics teams are merging. “Everyone is now a mixed-methods researcher” would overstate the evidence.

A segment hypothesis, not a winning market

GRIT’s 2026 supplier analysis describes a particularly strong operating profile among service firms with 101–500 staff, infrastructure constraints for small specialists, and a managed transition for the largest firms. That analysis mixes survey results with advisers’ interpretation, so it should shape a hypothesis rather than settle one. Meanwhile ESOMAR’s buyer research places in-house execution at around half of projects for several team structures, with wide variation by sector (30% in-house in FMCG, 23% in pharmaceuticals).10,28 The in-house/agency line is a handoff pattern, not a binary.

Primary hypothesis

A small or mid-sized agency running recurring quantitative studies or trackers, with several people touching each project and clients expecting editable PowerPoint and Excel. Enough repeat work that mistakes and rework are expensive; not enough engineering to build a connected workflow. Inference

Secondary hypothesis

The client-side team that receives work from several agencies and must interrogate, reuse, and defend it internally. MRS/AURA’s ClientSight 2025 found growing impact and influence were both top priorities and top challenges for these professionals.29 Industry survey

Section 03 · A study is a chain of decisions and handoffs

Eight moments where meaning can quietly change.

Triangulated from current job descriptions, platform documentation, and disclosure standards. It is a workflow model to validate, not a measured time-use study.

Moment
The researcher’s real task
Frequent failure or cost
01Brief & proposal
Turn a business decision into a population, measures, method, timeline, and deliverable.
A vague question survives until reporting; unpromising pitches consume scarce capacity.
02Questionnaire & programming
Define routing, response options, quotas, and what each measure will mean.
The programmed instrument diverges from the approved questionnaire.
03Fieldwork
Monitor sample, quotas, incidence, and quality.
Fraud, poor answers, composition shifts, and late changes — including undisclosed top-up sample.
04Preparation
Reconcile files, labels, missing codes, multiple responses, exclusions, and weights.
A plausible-looking dataset carries a wrong denominator or a silent recode.
05Exploration
Work through tables, segments, waves, tests, and open ends.
Many interesting cuts, small bases, uncertain relevance, lost reasoning.
06Interpretation
Decide which findings matter, test alternatives, write caveats.
The attractive result beats the robust one; prose outlives changed evidence.
07Delivery
Check tables, build slides and workbooks, answer client questions.
Repeated copying, inconsistent figures, hard-to-reconstruct numbers.
08Next wave
Reuse definitions and outputs with a changed questionnaire or sample.
A refreshed chart appears comparable when the measure is not.

The small details are part of the result. AAPOR’s Transparency Initiative requires population, full question wording and answer options, sample construction, mode, weighting, and more to be disclosed with findings, and further details within 30 days of a request.24 Its June 2026 code extends this to processing, quality checks, exclusions, and AI use. In survey work, source-to-claim traceability is a professional obligation before it is a product feature.

Where the quality work actually lands. In the Global Data Quality benchmark led by the Insights Association, sample providers reported removing 0.8% of respondents in-survey for fraud and quality reasons; research agencies reported 3.3%.19 Industry survey The gap suggests that consequential quality judgments are made downstream, by the agency, after data arrives — exactly the moment Velocity’s preparation microscope targets. Inference

What the voices add up to

Researchers welcome speed but define their professional value as authorship and accountability. Their anxieties are specific: confident output that hides uncertainty, reasoning that cannot be recovered later, comparisons that silently stop being comparable, and juniors who cannot tell when something is wrong. A product that makes these visible serves the researcher’s identity rather than threatening it. Inference

Quotations were captured from search-indexed excerpts of the named sources because direct page access was blocked in this research environment. Check wording against the originals before external use. Forum voices (Reddit, Hacker News, LinkedIn) could not be accessed and remain a gap.

Reading across the surveys

The gap between use (roughly 50–98%) and embedding (roughly 10%) is the most consistent pattern across sources, and the most relevant to Velocity. Researchers have added AI to steps; they have not rebuilt the workflow around it, because they cannot yet verify it cheaply. Verification cost, not capability, is the bottleneck — which is precisely where a product built around inspectable evidence could earn its place. Inference

Pressure predates AI. An MRS qualitative study of supply-side talent (2022) described long hours, manual work, and researchers stretching to protect client quality; this pass could not re-locate it, so treat it as evidence of longstanding dynamics, not a measured 2026 rate. A product that simply lets agencies take on more projects may intensify that strain. The desirable experience is less reconstruction and checking, and more time to reason and speak confidently to a client.

Section 06 · Clean data is a decision, not a button

Three reasonable cleaning rules. Three different answers.

Pew Research Center’s August 2026 study screened one opt-in survey three ways. Removing suspect respondents generally lowered error, but no method reliably solved the problem — and the choice of method moved results.

Same 11,114 respondents, four analysis populations
Left: respondents retained by each screen. Right: Harris’s lead among Hispanic voters in the resulting sample, percentage points.
Source: Pew Research Center, “No Easy Fix for Bogus Respondents in Online Opt-In Polls,” 27 Aug 2026; online opt-in survey of 11,114 US adults, 14–19 Nov 2024, sample via CloudResearch from Cint’s Lucid marketplace.1,2 Measured

Why it matters for Velocity

Pew also found bogus respondents disproportionately claim to be young or Hispanic, producing subgroup errors nearly twice the general-population error in an earlier benchmark.1 A quality rule is therefore a substantive analytical choice that changes who a percentage describes. It deserves the same inspection and provenance as a weight.

This directly supports PR #80’s inspect a flag → see its effect → then decide pattern, and argues against any “one-click clean.” Inference

The tracker version of the same problem

Tracker guidance converges on one rule: keep core wording, translations, sample sources, and composition identical wave to wave, record every deliberate change, and annotate charts when something did change.18 Practitioner voice

Quirks contributors describe fraud from bad actors, bots, and AI imitation as an existential threat, compounded by long, repetitive surveys that degrade even honest answers.20 The study was discussed widely, including on Columbia’s statistics blog.3

Section 07 · Can anyone reconstruct the number?

Even with the code in hand, only half the results came back.

The most direct evidence on analytical memory comes from academic survey research, where documentation norms are strongest. That makes it a conservative benchmark for commercial work.

Krähmer, Schächtele and Auspurg audited more than 1,000 articles built on the European Social Survey (2015–2020). Only about 35% of authors shared code. For 100 articles with code available, they attempted to reproduce 699 published results from raw data.16 Measured

Why this is relevant to agencies. Commercial projects rarely publish code, frequently change hands, and must answer client questions weeks after delivery. If a disciplined academic workflow loses a quarter of its results to missing materials, the agency equivalent — “which base was that, and who applied the exclusion?” — is plausibly worse. This is inference; no equivalent audit of commercial research was found. Inference

Implication

A durable record of definition, transformation, and judgment attached to each claim is not a nice-to-have audit feature. It is the thing most likely to be missing when a client disputes a number or a new wave arrives. The unit Velocity should preserve is not the table or the chart; it is the answer and its reasons.

“Easy crosstabs” and “traceable AI” are both taken.

Researchers cross boundaries between collection platforms, processing teams or files, analysis tools, and presentation software. The examples below establish capabilities, not market share.

Where the competitive bar actually is

Every competitor can demonstrate a clean first answer on a well-behaved file. Few demonstrations show what happens to edited prose, approved caveats, and exported slides when the population, weight, exclusion, or wave changes. That late-change path is where Velocity’s claim should be tested — and against a capable baseline such as Displayr/Q or the researcher’s own stack, not a strawman. Inference

Section 09 · A proposed position for Velocity

Findings researchers can explain, challenge, revise, and stand behind.

Broader than “analysis software,” narrower and more credible than “an AI researcher.” The unit of value is a defensible answer to a research question.

1Intent

The client question and the decision it informs.

2Definition

Population, eligibility, numerator, denominator, missing policy, weighting, comparable waves.

3Evidence

Computed result, bases, relevant alternatives, quality concerns, source references.

4Judgment

The researcher’s interpretation, caveat, and review decision — in their words.

5Delivery state

Which slides, workbooks, or shared answers depend on it; what is due for review after a change.

This is substantially aligned with PR #80’s exploration and the reviewed handoff in PRs #75 and #79. The added emphasis is the late change. A researcher discovers the product’s value when a client changes the target population, a weight is corrected, a suspect batch is investigated, or wave five contains a new response code: can they see the affected claims, keep their own reasoning, and repair the deliverable without starting over?

No moat claimed

The prototypes use synthetic fixtures and interaction checks; there is no researcher usability result or measured correction burden, and the research-evaluation PRs are explicit about missing human ratings. The defensible claim today is a promising, unusually coherent product hypothesis with good prototype coverage of its hardest decisions. Lead with researcher competence — “Get from a messy survey file to an answer you can explain and revise” — and avoid “instant insights” or “replace the researcher.”

Section 10 · The next exploration should follow real work

Observe six to eight recent projects, not opinions about AI.

Split participants between agency research leads, data/operations staff, and client-side recipients. For each, ask for a real questionnaire or codebook, received data, analysis file, delivered deck, and one revision or client query — redacted where needed.

Reconstruct what happened, then have the researcher perform four tasks in both their current stack and the relevant prototype:

TaskWhat to observe
Explain a disputed percentageFiles opened, people contacted, time to find the exact base, and whether the explanation is correct.
Investigate a quality exclusion or weight changeWhether they inspect effects before applying a rule; what they record about the decision. (Pew’s design is a ready-made template.)
Revise a delivered headlineWhich numbers, prose, charts, and caveats they remember to update — and which they miss.
Bring in the next tracker waveHow they detect changes in wording, routing, codes, sample source, and comparability. Include one deliberately incompatible change.

Record

  • Rework and wrong assumptions caught
  • Handoffs and people contacted
  • Time spent checking vs reasoning
  • Confidence explaining the result, and whether that confidence was warranted
  • False assurances and unsupported suggestions from the tool

Don’t rely on

  • Task completion alone
  • Preference for a screen
  • General attitudes to AI — six surveys already cover these
  • Comparison against a weak baseline; include Displayr/Q or the participant’s own stack
What would prove the hypothesis wrong

If researchers using the prototype make as many consequential corrections, miss as many stale claims after a change, or take longer to reach a defensible answer than in their current stack, the position fails — regardless of how much they like the interface. If the added definition work slows common tasks for experienced Q/Displayr users without catching more errors, narrow to a preparation-and-review integration instead.

The research question is now sharp: can one researcher carry a defensible answer through a change with less reconstruction and less risk than they can today? If yes, in real projects, that is a compelling researcher-oriented wedge for agencies and their clients alike.
